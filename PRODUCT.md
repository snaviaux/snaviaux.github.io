# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The portfolio serves three overlapping audiences:

- Hiring leaders and recruiters evaluating Steven for senior infrastructure, security, identity, platform, or AI-operations work.
- Consulting and project leads with regulated AI, identity, cloud, or operational problems that need an experienced operator.
- Technical peers looking for useful field notes, working-system evidence, and an honest operating model.

Visitors often arrive on a phone, scan before they read, and need to understand both the shape of Steven's work and whether the claims are supported. The shared job is deciding whether a conversation with Steven is worth starting.

## Product Purpose

StevenNaviaux.com establishes professional trust through specific operating principles, measurable outcomes, selected builds, and writing grounded in systems that actually run. Its primary conversion is a qualified conversation through the contact form or LinkedIn. Reading a build or field note is supporting evidence on the way to that conversation, not an end in itself.

Success means a visitor can quickly answer three questions: what Steven does, what proves he can do it, and how to contact him. The preferred journey is signal, evidence, then conversation.

## Brand Personality

Grounded, operational, quietly opinionated.

The voice should feel like a capable engineer walking someone through a system with the logs open: confident without boasting, candid about failure, precise without becoming sterile, and occasionally wry. The desired emotions are trust, curiosity, and the relief of finding someone who has stayed for the difficult operational part.

## Anti-references

- Glossy SaaS marketing that substitutes polish for evidence.
- Generic AI hype, glowing gradients, and vague claims about transformation.
- Resume-wall layouts that make visitors decode a chronology before they understand the work.
- Terminal cosplay or developer aesthetics used as decoration rather than as evidence of a real operating model.
- Hollow dashboard metrics, vanity counters, or interactions that cannot explain what changed because of them.
- Dense technical disclosure that exposes private infrastructure or turns credibility into an attack surface.

## Design Principles

1. **Receipts before claims.** Pair important assertions with measurable outcomes, public writing, sanitized artifacts, or a direct path to request a walkthrough.
2. **Operation is the product.** Emphasize ownership, recovery, cost, governance, and the long tail after the first successful demo.
3. **One path to conversation.** LinkedIn and the contact form are the primary conversions. Other links should build enough confidence to use them.
4. **Make complexity scannable.** Strong hierarchy, concise labels, and progressive detail should make a substantial body of work usable on a phone.
5. **Private by design.** Show the shape and consequences of the work without exposing hostnames, network paths, secrets, or private dashboards.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Treat 390px mobile use as a first-class operating context, with at least 44 by 44px touch targets, keyboard-complete navigation, visible focus, logical heading order, and no state communicated by color alone. Respect reduced-motion preferences, preserve useful content when JavaScript or partial requests fail, and keep forms labeled with specific sending, success, and error feedback.
